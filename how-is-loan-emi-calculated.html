How is a loan EMI or monthly payment calculated?

A loan payment becomes easier to understand when you separate the amount borrowed, the interest rate and the time you have to repay it.

By CarrowmontUpdated 19 Oct 2026Educational guideMethodology

30-second answer

A fixed-rate loan payment is mainly driven by the amount borrowed, the interest rate and the number of payments. Each payment covers interest on the balance that is still owed and reduces part of the principal.

What does EMI mean?

EMI means equated monthly instalment. It is a common term in India for a regular monthly loan payment. In many other countries, people simply say monthly loan payment. The maths can be the same when the loan has a fixed rate and equal monthly payments.

The payment formula in plain language

A standard amortizing loan spreads principal and interest across the chosen term. The payment formula uses the loan amount, the monthly interest rate and the number of monthly payments.

Fixed monthly paymentPayment = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1)P is the amount borrowed, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of monthly payments.

If the interest rate is zero, the payment is simply the amount borrowed divided by the number of payments.

Why is more interest usually paid early?

Interest is calculated on the outstanding balance. Early in the loan, that balance is larger, so a bigger part of the payment usually goes to interest. As the balance falls, the interest part usually falls too and more of the same payment goes toward principal.

A simple example

Suppose a loan is 100,000, the annual rate is 8%, and the term is 5 years with monthly payments. In a standard fixed-rate model, the monthly payment is about 2,028. Across 60 payments, the modeled total interest is about 21,658.

A lender's actual figure can differ because of fees, daily-interest rules, payment dates, insurance, taxes, variable rates, rounding or product-specific terms. A calculator is a planning estimate, not a lender statement.

What changes the monthly payment?

  • Borrow more: The payment rises.
  • Use a higher interest rate: The payment rises and total interest usually rises.
  • Choose a longer term: The monthly payment usually falls, but interest may be paid for longer.
  • Choose a shorter term: The monthly payment usually rises, but total interest may fall.

Why can two calculators show different answers?

They may use different compounding rules, payment timing, fees, rounding or rate conventions. Some loans use daily interest. Some include fees in the amount financed. Check the assumptions before comparing only the final payment.

What should you compare before taking a loan?

  1. Check the amount you really need to borrow.
  2. Compare the interest rate and, where relevant, the APR or equivalent total-cost measure.
  3. Look at both the monthly payment and the total interest over the full term.
  4. Check fees, penalties and whether the rate can change.
  5. Make sure the payment still leaves room for essential spending and emergency savings.

Monthly payment is only one part of affordability

A loan can be mathematically affordable in the calculator and still feel uncomfortable in real life. The monthly payment has to sit beside housing costs, food, transport, insurance, existing debt, emergency savings and other goals. Before taking a loan, look at what is left after the payment rather than asking only whether the payment fits under a lender limit.

A useful check is to test the payment against a normal month and a difficult month. If one unexpected bill would force you to borrow again, the loan may leave too little breathing room even when the EMI itself looks manageable.

When the standard formula may not match your actual loan

The formula on this page is for a fixed-rate loan with equal monthly payments. Real products can work differently. Interest may be calculated daily, the rate may change, a first or final payment may be unusual, fees may be financed, or insurance and taxes may be collected with the payment. Some lenders also round amounts differently.

Use the calculator to understand the core repayment maths, then compare the result with the lender's written schedule. If the two numbers differ, find out which assumption is different before making a decision. The lender's contract and repayment schedule control the actual loan.

Frequently asked questions

Is EMI the same as interest?

No. An EMI or monthly payment normally contains both principal and interest.

Does a lower EMI always mean a cheaper loan?

No. A longer term can reduce the monthly payment while increasing the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

Does Carrowmont decide whether I qualify for a loan?

No. Carrowmont models payment maths from the assumptions you enter. A lender decides eligibility and approval using its own rules.
