SIP PLANNING IN INDIA

How much SIP is needed to build ₹1 crore?

The required SIP depends strongly on how long you have and what return assumption you use. Time can change the monthly amount dramatically.

A HIGH-INTENT INDIA QUESTION

There is no single SIP amount for ₹1 crore.

The monthly SIP depends on the time horizon, expected return assumption, contribution timing and whether the SIP increases over time. The same ₹1 crore target can require a very different starting amount over 10 years versus 25 years.

Illustrative monthly SIP for a ₹1 crore target at a 12% annual return assumption10 years: about ₹45,059 per month15 years: about ₹21,211 per month20 years: about ₹10,974 per month25 years: about ₹5,930 per month

These figures use Carrowmont's methodology: the annual assumption is converted to an equivalent monthly compound rate, contributions are made at month-end, there is no existing lump sum, and there is no annual step-up.

Why the amount falls so much when the horizon gets longer

A longer horizon gives you more monthly contributions and gives the earlier contributions more time to compound. The extra time does two jobs at once. This is why starting earlier can matter even when the monthly amount is modest.

But ₹1 crore is a nominal target

If the goal is many years away, ask what ₹1 crore is intended to buy. At 5% annual inflation, a basket costing ₹1 crore today would cost about ₹2.65 crore in 20 years. So a nominal ₹1 crore target in 20 years may represent much less purchasing power than ₹1 crore today.

Important: return assumptions are not guaranteed. SEBI's investor calculators explicitly note that stock-market returns are not fixed and calculator results are illustrations.

What if you already have money invested?

Existing investments can reduce the required monthly SIP because they also have time to grow. A goal calculator should therefore include the existing amount rather than ignoring it.

What if you increase the SIP every year?

A step-up SIP can lower the starting monthly amount for the same target because later contributions become larger. That can be useful when income is expected to grow, but the future contribution level should still be affordable.

Should you assume 12%?

Treat 12% as an illustration, not a promise. Run the same goal at a lower return assumption. If the required SIP becomes unaffordable, the plan is sensitive to investment performance and may need more time, a larger starting contribution or a different target.

Frequently asked questions

Can a smaller SIP reach Rs 1 crore with more time?

Yes in a mathematical projection because more time means more contributions and more compounding periods. The actual outcome still depends on investment performance and the assumptions used.

Is 12% a guaranteed SIP return?

No. SIP is a contribution method, not a guaranteed-return product. The investment chosen determines actual returns, which can be higher, lower or negative over some periods.

Should the Rs 1 crore target be inflation-adjusted?

If Rs 1 crore represents the cost of something in today's money, yes, the future target should generally reflect expected price growth. If Rs 1 crore is simply a nominal future target, no extra inflation adjustment is required.
