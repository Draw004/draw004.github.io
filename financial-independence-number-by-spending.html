WHAT THE NUMBER MEANS

A transparent spending-based target

Your first-pass FI number is the annual spending the portfolio needs to fund divided by the planning withdrawal rate you select.

FI number todayAnnual portfolio-funded spending ÷ Planning withdrawal rate

If dependable pension, rental or other recurring income will cover part of your lifestyle, subtract that income from spending before using this quick reference.

Today's money vs future money

The today's-money result preserves today's purchasing-power frame. When you enter years until FI, Carrowmont also compounds annual spending by the inflation assumption and translates that future nominal spending into a future nominal FI target at the same withdrawal-rate assumption.

The future-money number is an illustration, not a forecast.

LIMITATIONS

Useful shortcut, not a complete financial plan

This page deliberately does not model your current portfolio, future contributions, investment returns, taxes, fees or the order of market returns.

FAQ

Questions about the FI number

What is a financial independence number?

It is a planning estimate of the invested portfolio that corresponds to the annual spending a portfolio needs to fund under a chosen withdrawal-rate assumption.

Is 25× annual spending always enough?

No. Twenty-five times annual spending is simply the reciprocal of a 4% starting withdrawal-rate assumption. Real-world sustainability depends on market returns, inflation, taxes, fees, spending flexibility and time horizon.

Why does a lower withdrawal rate create a larger FI number?

The formula divides annual portfolio-funded spending by the rate. A smaller denominator therefore creates a larger first-pass target.

Should I include income such as pension or rent?

Enter only the spending the portfolio needs to fund. If dependable income will cover part of your spending, subtract that amount first.

Should I use monthly or annual spending?

Use whichever is easier. Monthly inputs are multiplied by 12 internally, so equivalent monthly and annual amounts produce the same target.

Why is there a future-money FI number?

It helps separate a target in today's purchasing-power terms from the larger nominal number that could result if the same spending rises with inflation for several years.

Does this tool model investment returns?

No. It estimates the target implied by spending. Use the full FI Planner for current assets, recurring investments, returns and target age.

Does changing currency convert my spending?

No. Currency selection changes the unit and number format only. The numeric amount you enter is not converted using an exchange rate.
