Inflation & Purchasing-Power Monitor

Headline inflation is one number. Household budgets are not. This monitor separates the official U.S. CPI reading from the spending groups underneath it, then shows how price changes can alter purchasing power and why a household's own spending mix may feel different from the headline.

One inflation rate does not describe every part of a household budget

The BLS CPI is built from a broad market basket. In September 2026, transportation prices were 7.9% above a year earlier while medical care was 1.5% higher. Housing, the largest major group by relative importance, was up 3.3%.

Why the weights matter: The eight BLS major expenditure groups are mutually exclusive and sum to 100.001% because the published weights are rounded. They provide a clean way to illustrate how a different household spending mix can produce a different weighted rate without pretending to recreate the full official CPI methodology.

Not a personal CPI: This is a transparent weighted illustration, not an official inflation statistic or a forecast. The BLS uses detailed item indexes, sampling, expenditure weights, seasonal treatment and index methods that this simple weighted view does not reproduce. Your own prices, substitutions, geography, taxes and spending patterns can also differ.

Purchasing power compounds over longer periods

The latest annual inflation rate is useful for current conditions, but purchasing power is a cumulative question. The CPI-U all-items index rose from 241.428 in September 2016 to 336.940 in September 2026. On that index comparison, the amount needed in September 2026 to match $100 of September 2016 purchasing power is about $139.56.

Selected pressure points show why the headline can feel incomplete

Some categories sit far above or below the 3.7% headline. Energy was 20.4% higher than a year earlier, gasoline 34.2% higher and airline fares 22.4% higher, while used cars and trucks were 1.5% lower. These are selected overlapping categories, not pieces that should be added together.
