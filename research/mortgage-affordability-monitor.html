Mortgage Affordability Monitor

How much does today's mortgage-rate environment change the monthly payment on the same loan, and how does that compare with recent new-home prices and household income?

At the current 7.32% Freddie Mac 30-year fixed reference rate, a standardized $400,000 mortgage produces about $2,748/month in principal and interest — roughly $300 more per month than at the comparable 6.19% rate one year earlier.

The rate moved more than the home-price reference

The current 30-year fixed PMMS reference is 1.13 percentage points above the comparable Freddie Mac observation from 23 October 2025. Separately, Census/HUD reports the August 2026 median new-house sale price at $393,700; its current release shows that figure below the revised August 2025 comparison. The two series measure different things, but together they show why purchase-price changes alone do not describe financing affordability.

Median-new-home reference scenario

Using the latest $393,700 median new-house sale price, a standardized 20% down payment leaves a $314,960 mortgage. At 7.32% over 30 years, principal and interest are about $2,164 per month.

Against the Census 2025 median household income of $87,460, that P&I-only payment is about 29.7% of gross median monthly household income.

Important: This is not a lender debt-to-income ratio or qualification threshold. It excludes property tax, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA/strata charges, maintenance, closing costs and lender fees. It also compares national medians that do not describe any one household or local housing market.

Methodology and sources

The payment calculation uses the standard fully amortizing fixed-payment formula over 360 monthly payments. Official reported data and Carrowmont calculations are kept separate.

  • Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey (PMMS) — 30-year fixed-rate reference; current observation 22 Oct 2026.
  • U.S. Census Bureau / HUD New Residential Sales — median sales price of new houses; latest available period Aug 2026.
  • U.S. Census Bureau, Income in the United States: 2025 — median household income.
  • FRED series MSPNHSUS — convenient access to the revised Census/HUD monthly home-price series used for the trend table.
