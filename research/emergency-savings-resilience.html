What $10,000 actually covers depends on the monthly cash shortfall

A reserve does not have one fixed “months” value. If some dependable income continues, the reserve only needs to cover the gap between essential outflows and remaining income. With a $10,000 accessible reserve, a $1,500 monthly shortfall lasts about 6.7 months; a $5,000 shortfall lasts only 2 months.

Federal Reserve evidence: Monthly budget margin and emergency savings move together

SHED does not say that monthly surplus automatically causes a larger emergency fund, but the association is stark. Only 13% of adults who said they never had money left over at month-end reported three months of savings, compared with 86% of those who always had money left over.

How large an emergency expense could savings handle?

The 2025 SHED asked adults for the largest emergency expense they could handle using only savings. Thirty-eight percent selected $5,000 or more, while 30% selected less than $500.

“Three months” is not one universal dollar amount

Three months of essential outflow is $4,500 for a household needing $1,500 a month, but $15,000 for a household needing $5,000. That is why a useful target starts with the household's own essential cash burn rather than a fixed headline number.

Illustrative income-loss scenario
