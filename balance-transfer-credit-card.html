Credit-card balance transfer: When is moving the debt actually worth it?

A lower promotional rate can help, but only when the fee, repayment pace, post-promotion rate and card behaviour all work together.

By CarrowmontPublished 18 November 2026 · Reviewed 19 November 2026Educational guideEditorial Policy
Want to build the repayment path?Compare balance-transfer offer costs→

Compare the promotional APR, transfer fee, payment, rate after the promotion and total costs against keeping your card.

30-second answer

A balance transfer is worth considering when the interest you are likely to avoid is greater than the transfer fee and any other costs, and you can realistically repay the transferred balance before the promotional rate ends. A 0% headline rate does not mean the transfer is free.

Promotional periods, fees, eligibility rules, payment allocation and consumer protections differ by country and issuer. Read the exact card terms before transferring a balance.

What a balance transfer actually does

A balance transfer moves existing debt from one credit card or lender to another card, often at a temporary lower rate. It does not erase the debt. It changes the financing terms and, ideally, creates a window in which more of each payment reduces principal rather than interest.

The strategy works best when the transfer is paired with a repayment plan. Without that plan, a borrower can simply move debt, pay a fee, then reach the end of the promotional period with a large balance still outstanding.

The four numbers that decide whether the offer is useful

  1. Transfer fee: Usually charged as a percentage or fixed amount when the balance is moved.
  2. Promotional rate: The rate applied to the transferred balance during the offer period.
  3. Promotional period: How long the lower rate lasts.
  4. Post-promotion rate: The rate that applies to any remaining balance after the offer ends.

Also check the maximum amount that can be transferred, minimum monthly payment, whether purchases receive the same rate, and what happens if a payment is late.

Break-even: Compare the fee with the interest avoided

Simple transfer costTransfer fee + promotional interest + interest after the offer on any unpaid balance
Potential benefitInterest under the old repayment path − Total transfer cost

The old repayment path matters. If you would have repaid the original card in two months, paying a 4% transfer fee might save very little. If the balance would otherwise remain at a high rate for a year or more, the same fee can be much easier to recover.

Worked example: 10,000 moved to a 0% offer

Assume a 10,000 balance currently charges 24% APR. A new card offers 0% on transferred balances for 12 months with a 3% transfer fee. Ignore taxes and other card fees for simplicity.

PathMonthly payment to clear in 12 monthsApproximate financing cost
Keep 24% cardAbout 946About 1,347 interest
Transfer at 0% + 3% feeAbout 858300 transfer fee

In this simplified case, the transfer lowers the financing cost by roughly 1,047 if the promotional terms remain valid and the full transferred amount is cleared in 12 months. The comparison changes quickly if the fee is higher, the old debt would have been cleared sooner, or a balance remains when the post-promotion rate begins.

The payment target matters more than the minimum payment

For a 10,000 transfer with a 300 fee and 12 months at 0%, the planning payment is about 10,300 ÷ 12 = 858 per month. A card’s required minimum payment may be much lower. Paying only the minimum can leave a large balance after the promotional window.

Set the repayment payment from the deadline backward. If 858 is not affordable, the offer may not solve the underlying cash-flow problem. A longer promotional period, smaller transfer or broader debt plan may be needed.

New purchases can break the simplicity

A transfer card can have different rates for transferred debt and new spending. Purchases may not receive the promotional rate, and payment-allocation rules can be complicated. The cleanest behavioural setup is often to treat the transfer card as a repayment vehicle rather than a new spending limit, unless the terms clearly support another use.

Watch the post-promotion cliff

The promotional rate is temporary. If 4,000 remains when a high standard rate starts, the economics can deteriorate quickly. Build a monthly checkpoint: Remaining balance, months left and required monthly payment. If the required payment starts rising beyond your cash flow, adjust early rather than waiting for the deadline.

When a balance transfer is less likely to help

  • The transfer fee is large relative to the interest you would avoid.
  • You cannot clear most or all of the balance before the promotional period ends.
  • The new credit limit is too small to move enough of the high-rate debt.
  • The transfer causes you to keep spending on the old card and rebuild the balance.
  • You are repeatedly transferring debt without fixing the monthly cash-flow shortfall.
  • The old debt is already near payoff, so there is little interest left to save.

Decision checklist before applying

  1. Write down the current balance, APR and expected payoff date without the transfer.
  2. Calculate the transfer fee in money, not only as a percentage.
  3. Confirm the promotional rate and exact end date.
  4. Check the standard rate after the promotion and the rate on new purchases.
  5. Calculate the monthly payment needed to be at zero before the offer ends.
  6. Check whether that payment fits your budget with a margin for irregular expenses.
  7. Read the consequences of late or missed payments.
  8. Plan what happens to the old card so the debt does not quietly rebuild.

Frequently asked questions

Can a 0% balance transfer still charge a fee?

Yes. Regulators such as the U.S. CFPB explicitly note that a transfer fee can be charged even on a 0% interest offer. The fee belongs in the break-even calculation.

Should I transfer as much as possible?

Not automatically. Transfer the amount that improves the financing cost and that you can repay under the promotional timeline. Moving more debt without a feasible payment plan can simply postpone the problem.

Does a balance transfer reduce the debt balance?

No. It changes where the balance sits and what it costs to finance. The balance falls only when you make payments or receive a credit under the terms.

What if I cannot clear it before the promotion ends?

Model the remaining balance at the post-promotion rate before accepting the offer. If that result is still unaffordable, the transfer alone is not a complete solution.
