COMPOUNDING & TIME GUIDE

How much difference can starting 5 or 10 years earlier make?

Starting earlier adds both more contributions and more compounding time, which can materially change a long-term projection.

BEFORE YOU START

Starting earlier changes two things at once.

You make more contributions, and the earliest contributions have more time to grow. That is why the difference can become larger than the extra money contributed.

Time is especially valuable when the goal is far away.

The later years of a long projection can contain a larger share of growth because returns are being applied to a bigger accumulated balance.

Starting later does not make planning pointless.

A shorter horizon simply means the other levers become more important: contribution size, goal amount, contribution increases and the date of the goal.

Compare contributions as well as ending values.

A longer period also means more of your own money has been contributed. Separate that from modelled growth so the result remains understandable.

Frequently asked questions

Should I assume a higher return because I start late?

Increasing the return assumption can make the maths look easier but does not create a more reliable plan. It is usually more informative to change controllable inputs such as time or contributions.

Does starting earlier always produce more money?

In a constant-return mathematical model with the same monthly contribution, a longer period produces a higher projected value. Real investment results are not constant or guaranteed.
