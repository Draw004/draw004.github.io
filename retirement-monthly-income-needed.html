BEFORE YOU START

Start with the spending that will still exist after work ends.

Retirement income needs are usually easier to understand as a monthly budget than as one large portfolio number. Begin with the expenses you expect to keep, remove costs that are likely to end, and add costs that may rise or begin later.

Do not assume every expense moves in the same direction.

Commuting or mortgage costs may fall. Travel may rise in early retirement. Healthcare can become more important later. The useful question is not simply what you spend today, but which parts of that spending will still need funding.

Subtract dependable retirement income separately.

If you expect pension income or another dependable cash flow, model it separately from portfolio withdrawals. That shows the amount your savings may actually need to fund rather than treating the whole lifestyle as an investment withdrawal.

Inflation connects today's budget with the future budget.

A monthly budget stated in today's currency should be grown to the retirement date before comparing it with future income and assets.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use my salary as retirement income?

Salary is not the same as spending. Retirement planning is usually clearer when you start from expenses and then add a buffer for uncertainty.

Should I include one-time goals?

Large one-time costs such as a home renovation, travel or family support are easier to understand when modelled separately from recurring monthly expenses.
