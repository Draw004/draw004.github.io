CREDIT-CARD BASICS

How does credit-card interest build up?

Follow the balance from purchase to statement, interest and repayment without assuming every card uses the same rules.

By CarrowmontPublished and reviewed 20 October 2026Educational guideEditorial Policy
MODEL A REPAYMENTOpen the Loan / EMI Calculator→

Use a fixed-loan illustration to see how rate, payment and term interact. A revolving credit card can calculate interest differently, so check the card agreement.

30-second answer

Credit-card interest usually applies when a balance is carried beyond the conditions of an interest-free grace period. The issuer may calculate interest from daily or average balances and may use different rates for purchases, cash advances and balance transfers. Making only the minimum payment can keep the balance alive for much longer because part of each payment goes to interest and fees rather than principal.

Grace periods, minimum-payment formulas, disclosures and payment-allocation rules differ by issuer and country. The example below is a simple teaching estimate, not a reproduction of every card agreement.

Start with the balances shown on the statement

TermPlain-language meaning
Current balanceWhat the account shows as owed now, including activity after the last statement.
Statement balanceThe balance captured when the billing cycle closed.
Minimum paymentThe smallest required amount to keep the account from being treated as unpaid under the agreement. It is not a promise of quick or low-cost repayment.
Due dateThe date by which the required payment must normally be received.
Credit limitThe maximum revolving amount approved, not an amount the household can necessarily afford to owe.

If you pay only the statement minimum while continuing to spend, the current balance may not fall much. Always distinguish the amount due, the balance attracting interest and new transactions that have not yet reached a statement.

From annual rate to daily or periodic interest

A card normally states an annual rate or APR. The issuer may convert that annual rate into a daily or monthly periodic rate, apply it to a balance measure, and add the resulting interest according to the agreement. Some issuers use a daily balance; others may use an average daily balance or another permitted method.

A rough monthly shortcut divides an annual rate by 12, but that is only an illustration. Daily compounding, the number of days in the cycle, transaction dates, payment dates and different rate buckets can make the actual statement different.

What is a grace period?

A purchase grace period is the time in which eligible purchases may avoid interest if the required balance is paid in full and on time. Not every card or transaction has the same grace period. Cash advances commonly begin attracting interest immediately, and balance transfers may have separate rates and fees.

If a carried balance causes the grace period to be lost, new purchases may begin attracting interest under the agreement. Paying only the minimum is usually not enough to preserve a full-payment grace period.

Different transactions can have different prices

TransactionWhat to check
PurchasesPurchase APR, grace-period conditions and when interest starts.
Cash advancesSeparate rate, transaction fee and whether interest starts immediately.
Balance transfersTransfer fee, promotional rate, expiry date and standard rate afterwards.
Instalment plansPlan fee, effective cost, missed-payment consequences and what happens to rewards or returns.

Worked example: How much of the payment reaches principal?

Assume a balance of 100,000 and an annual rate of 30%. For a simple monthly illustration, 30% divided by 12 is about 2.5% for the first month.

StepIllustrative amount
Opening balance100,000
Approx. first-month interest at 2.5%2,500
If the payment is 5,000About 2,500 covers interest and about 2,500 reduces principal, before fees or new spending.
If the payment is 10,000About 2,500 covers interest and about 7,500 reduces principal, before fees or new spending.

The larger payment reduces the balance more quickly, so less principal remains to attract future interest. But the real statement may use a daily rate, different dates and separate transaction buckets. Use the example to understand direction, then read the actual agreement and statement.

Why minimum payments can keep debt alive

A minimum-payment formula may be a small percentage of the balance, a fixed floor, interest plus fees, or another local formula. When the balance falls, the minimum may fall too. That can slow repayment because the amount reaching principal becomes smaller.

Many statements show a warning or estimate of how long repayment could take if only minimums are paid. Read that information. A payment can be “on time” yet still create a long and expensive repayment path.

New spending while carrying a balance

New purchases make the moving target larger. Even if the monthly payment feels substantial, the balance can stay flat or rise when new spending plus interest exceeds the payment. Separating the card used for existing debt from daily spending may make the repayment path easier to see, but the right approach depends on access to essential spending and the terms of the account.

How payments may be allocated

If a card has balances at different rates, the issuer's rules and local law determine where the minimum and any extra payment go. An extra payment may be directed toward the highest-rate balance in some systems, while the minimum can be allocated differently. Do not assume the balance-transfer promotion is being repaid first; check the statement and agreement.

Read one statement carefully

  1. Find the statement balance, current balance and minimum due.
  2. Identify every interest rate and which transactions it applies to.
  3. Check the grace-period wording and whether it currently applies.
  4. Look for cash-advance, balance-transfer, annual, late and over-limit fees.
  5. See how the issuer calculates the balance used for interest.
  6. Check the due date and how long electronic or other payments take to arrive.
  7. Look for the minimum-payment repayment estimate or warning.
  8. Confirm how promotional rates end and what rate follows.

Ways to reduce future interest without hiding essentials

  • Stop adding optional purchases to a carried balance where practical.
  • Pay more than the minimum when the household budget safely allows it.
  • Pay earlier if the issuer's daily-balance method makes timing relevant.
  • Direct extra money toward the highest-cost debt after protecting minimums and priority bills.
  • Ask the issuer about hardship options before missing payments if repayment is becoming difficult.
  • Compare any balance-transfer or consolidation offer after fees, term and post-promotion rate.

Do not use emergency rent, food, medicine or essential utility money simply to make an extra card payment. A debt plan should fit the household's real cash flow.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking “minimum” means affordable: It only means the minimum required under the agreement.
  • Assuming every purchase has an interest-free period: Grace-period conditions and transaction types matter.
  • Ignoring cash-advance rules: These often use a higher rate and no purchase grace period.
  • Looking at the rate but not fees: Transfer and annual fees add real cost.
  • Continuing new spending without measuring it: Payments may not outrun interest plus purchases.
  • Using one simple formula as an exact statement forecast: Issuer methods vary.

Questions to ask before using or transferring a card balance

  • Which rate applies to purchases, cash advances and transfers?
  • What must I pay to keep the purchase grace period?
  • What is the transfer fee and when does the promotion end?
  • How is the minimum payment calculated?
  • How are payments allocated across rate buckets?
  • What happens after a late or missed payment?
  • Can I repay the balance without using new credit for essentials?

Frequently asked questions

Will paying the minimum avoid interest?

Usually no. It may keep the account from being treated as unpaid, but a carried balance can continue attracting interest.

Is APR divided by 12 the exact monthly interest?

It is a rough teaching shortcut. Real cards may use daily rates, daily balances and different transaction dates.

Does closing the card stop interest?

Closing may prevent new use, but the existing balance normally remains payable under the agreement and can continue attracting interest.
