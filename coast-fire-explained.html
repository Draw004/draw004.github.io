What is Coast FIRE and how is it different from full financial independence?

Coast FIRE describes a point where current invested assets may be enough to grow toward a future retirement target without further retirement contributions, under the assumptions used.

Direct answer

Coast FIRE means having enough invested today that, if the portfolio grows as assumed and you make no further retirement contributions, it could reach the retirement target by the chosen retirement age. You may still need earned income to cover current living costs; the concept removes the modeled need for additional retirement saving, not the need to pay today’s bills.

Coast FIRE is not the same as being financially independent today.

At full financial independence, the portfolio is intended to support current or near-term living expenses without relying on continued work income. At Coast FIRE, the retirement component may be on track under the model, but current expenses still need to be funded by work or another source.

The concept is a present-value question

Illustrative Coast amount today = future retirement target / (1 + assumed growth rate) ^ years remaining

That equation is only as good as its assumptions. The future target, growth rate, inflation basis, taxes, fees and time horizon must be internally consistent.

Illustration using index unitsFuture retirement target at the chosen age: 100 unitsYears remaining: 25Assumed annual growth: 6%Present amount that would grow to 100 under that constant assumption: about 23.3 units

Why the number can move a lot

  • A lower return assumption raises the amount needed today.
  • A shorter time horizon raises the amount needed today.
  • A higher future spending target raises the Coast amount.
  • If future spending rises with lifestyle inflation, the target can move even after you thought you had reached the milestone.

Coast FIRE can be useful as a flexibility milestone

The concept can help answer a different question from traditional FIRE: instead of "Can I stop working?", it asks "Could I reduce or redirect future retirement contributions if the current portfolio stays on track?" That may create room for other goals, career changes or reduced hours, but it does not remove the need to fund present-day living costs.

Do not confuse a modelled milestone with a guarantee

Real investment returns are not constant. A Coast calculation should be revisited after large market changes, changes in spending, a different target age or a major change in contributions. Taxes and fees can also reduce growth.

This illustration assumes a ₹3 crore target expressed in today’s purchasing power and therefore uses real growth rates. If the target is a future nominal rupee amount, nominal return assumptions must be used instead. Mixing the two is one of the most common Coast FI errors.

Coast FI is extremely sensitive to time and return assumptions

The earlier the coast point, the more years the existing portfolio has to compound—and the more years there are for actual returns to deviate from the assumption. A person with 30 years remaining can have a much lower Coast amount than someone with 15 years, but also more model uncertainty.

The retirement target still has to be credible

A Coast calculation can look precise while resting on a weak retirement target. Before calculating the coast amount, model retirement spending, non-portfolio income, retirement duration, inflation and withdrawal strategy. If those assumptions change, the Coast point changes.

Coasting is not the same as de-risking

Reaching the mathematical Coast amount does not mean the portfolio can be put entirely in cash. The growth assumption depends on an asset allocation that carries risk. Conversely, keeping an aggressive portfolio solely to preserve an optimistic growth rate may be inconsistent with the investor’s capacity for loss.

Why continuing some contributions can still be valuable

  • It creates a buffer if returns disappoint.
  • It can fund a higher future lifestyle or earlier retirement.
  • It reduces dependence on a single return assumption.
  • It can compensate for periods of unemployment or contribution pauses later.
  • It may let the portfolio gradually de-risk while keeping the target intact.

Stress-test the coast decision

  1. Reduce the expected real return by 1–2 percentage points.
  2. Increase the retirement target for higher spending or a longer horizon.
  3. Model an immediate market decline before the coast period begins.
  4. Check whether taxes/fees are reflected in the growth assumption.
  5. Recalculate every year rather than treating Coast FI as a permanent status.

The Coast amount is a present value of a future liability

Thinking of it this way exposes the two biggest assumptions: the future retirement target and the discount/growth rate. If either is wrong, the Coast amount changes. A high real growth rate discounts the target aggressively and makes the required amount look deceptively small.

Continuing contributions buys insurance against model error

After reaching the projected Coast amount, even a modest ongoing contribution can create substantial margin over 20–30 years. That margin can absorb lower returns, higher retirement spending or a desire to retire earlier. “Coast” is therefore an option, not an instruction to set contributions to zero.

A career change is often the real objective

Many people use Coast FI to justify moving from high-pay/high-stress work to lower-pay work that covers current expenses. Model the new cash flow carefully: if the new job does not fully cover living costs, portfolio withdrawals begin early and the strategy is no longer pure Coast FI.

Frequently asked questions

Does Coast FI mean I can stop working?

No. It means the retirement portfolio may no longer require additional contributions under the assumptions. Current living expenses still need to be funded unless you have other resources.

Should I use nominal or real returns?

Use real returns if the retirement target is stated in today’s purchasing power; use nominal returns if the target is a future nominal amount. Keep the basis consistent.

What happens if markets fall after I reach Coast FI?

Your Coast status can disappear. Recalculate from the new balance and remaining years rather than assuming the earlier label is permanent.

Is Coast FI a guaranteed milestone?

No. It is a projection based on a future return path and retirement target. Treat it as a planning checkpoint and stress-test it.
