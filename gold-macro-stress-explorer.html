    METHODOLOGY

    Six drivers, deterministic classifications, visible conflict

    Real yields and the U.S. dollar receive slightly higher weights because they are important financial conditions for a globally priced, non-yielding asset. Inflation, central-bank demand, fiscal stress and geopolitical/financial stress each retain meaningful influence.

    The engine classifies each driver from strong headwind to strong support, then combines those states using fixed weights. The score is used only to select a qualitative environment label.

    Internal frameworkReal yields × 1.5 + Dollar × 1.5 + four other drivers × 1.0Internal scores run from −2 to +2. They are not probabilities, returns or price targets.

    Why conflict matters

    High inflation, fiscal concern or geopolitical stress can coexist with high real yields and a strong dollar. The explorer keeps both sides visible instead of letting a simple majority vote create a false prediction.

    High inflation is not a guarantee

    Inflation can add support to gold’s macro environment, but real yields and dollar strength separately capture important offsetting forces.

    INDIA CONTEXT

    Global gold conditions and Indian gold prices are not the same thing

    Gold is globally priced in U.S. dollars, while Indian buyers experience the additional effect of USD/INR, import duties and taxes, local premiums and domestic demand. A globally supportive macro environment therefore does not translate one-for-one into an INR gold-price forecast.

    Use this as context, not allocation advice

    The explorer does not tell an Indian investor how much gold to hold. Portfolio fit depends on goals, liquidity needs, time horizon, other assets and risk tolerance.

    UNDERSTAND THE DRIVERS

    Why each force can matter

    Real yields

    Higher inflation-adjusted yields can raise the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. Falling or low real yields can reduce that headwind.

    U.S. dollar

    A stronger dollar can be a headwind for a globally dollar-priced metal, while dollar weakness can be supportive. The relationship is not mechanical.

    Inflation pressure

    Persistent inflation can support demand for stores of value, but inflation by itself does not guarantee higher gold prices.

    Central-bank demand

    Official-sector net buying can provide structural demand. Quarterly estimates can be revised, so Carrowmont keeps this driver human-reviewed.

    Fiscal stress

    Rising debt and interest burdens can increase attention on fiscal credibility and alternative stores of value, without implying default or currency collapse.

    Geopolitical / financial stress

    Periods of geopolitical or market stress can increase safe-haven demand, but the effect can compete with liquidity, yields and dollar moves.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    Does this tool predict the gold price?

    No. It produces a qualitative macro-environment classification and never estimates a future gold price, percentage return or buy/sell signal.

    Why are real yields and the dollar weighted more heavily?

    They are important financial conditions for a globally dollar-priced asset that does not itself pay interest. The weights are fixed, disclosed and protected by deterministic tests.

    Can inflation be supportive while the overall result is adverse?

    Yes. High real yields and a strong dollar can outweigh inflation support in the framework. That is one of the main lessons the explorer is designed to show.

    Are the source values live?

    No. Carrowmont uses a dated validated reference snapshot. The browser never calls the macro-data providers directly.

    Why is central-bank demand reviewed rather than automatically changed?

    Quarterly demand estimates can be revised. Human review helps preserve the source period and prevents a provisional estimate from being silently treated as final.

    Is this investment advice?

    No. It is educational macro scenario analysis and does not recommend a security, metal allocation, timing decision or trade.
