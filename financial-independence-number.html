FINANCIAL INDEPENDENCE GUIDE

What is your financial independence number?

Your financial independence number is a spending-based estimate of the portfolio that may be needed to support a chosen level of spending without relying on salary.

16
A SPENDING-BASED TARGET

Financial independence is easier to model from spending than from income.

The core question is not "How much do I earn?" but "How much would my portfolio need to provide each year if work income stopped?" That makes annual spending the natural starting point.

A simple mathematical shortcut is annual spending divided by a chosen withdrawal-rate assumption. At 4%, that is 25 times annual spending. At 3.5%, it is about 28.6 times. At 3%, it is about 33.3 times.

Example with annual spending of 60,000At 4%: target about 1.50 millionAt 3.5%: target about 1.71 millionAt 3%: target about 2.00 million

These are arithmetic scenarios, not recommended withdrawal rates. The sustainable amount depends on investment returns, inflation, taxes, fees, time horizon and how spending changes over time.

Why lower spending has a double effect

Reducing recurring spending can increase the amount available to save today and reduce the portfolio target needed later. That is why the savings rate is central to many financial-independence discussions.

Do not count every asset automatically

A home you plan to live in may be valuable but may not be available to fund spending unless you intend to sell, downsize or borrow against it. Separate assets that are genuinely available for financial independence from assets that serve another purpose.

Income after financial independence can reduce the target

Part-time work, pensions, rental income or other dependable cash flows can reduce the amount that the portfolio needs to provide. A good model lets you include those separately instead of pretending the portfolio must fund every expense forever.

Inflation still matters

If financial independence is years away, today's spending should be translated into future money or the model should work consistently in today's purchasing-power terms. Mixing the two can distort the target.

Use the number as a scenario, not a finish line. Test several spending levels and withdrawal assumptions so you can see what actually drives the target.

Financial independence is not identical to retirement

Someone can be financially independent and still choose to work. Retirement planning also tends to include a specific retirement age, pension timing, later-life healthcare costs and a finite planning horizon. The concepts overlap, but the questions are not identical.

Frequently asked questions

Is 25 times spending always enough?

No. It is simply the arithmetic equivalent of a 4% first-year withdrawal assumption. A different withdrawal assumption produces a different target, and real outcomes depend on inflation, returns, taxes, fees and time horizon.

Should I include my home in my FI number?

Only if the plan expects the home to help fund spending, for example through sale, downsizing or another deliberate strategy. A home you intend to keep may not function like an investable portfolio.

Can financial independence happen before retirement?

Yes. Financial independence describes the ability to fund a chosen lifestyle without relying on salary. Someone can reach that point and continue working by choice.
