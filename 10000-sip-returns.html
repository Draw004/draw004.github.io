SIP RETURNS GUIDE

₹10,000 Monthly Investment (SIP) for 10, 15, 20 and 25 years: what could it grow to?

A fixed monthly SIP can produce very different future values depending on time and the return assumption.

WANT TO START WITH YOUR NUMBERS?Try your monthly investment scenario→

Prefer to understand the assumptions first? Start with the basics below, then return to the calculator with more context.

BEFORE YOU START

Time changes the result more than most people expect.

Consider a fixed ₹10,000 Monthly Investment (SIP) with no existing investment and no annual increase. Using a 12% annual return assumption converted to an equivalent monthly compound rate, the projected values are:

₹10,000 Monthly Investment (SIP) at a 12% annual assumption10 years: about ₹22.19 lakh15 years: about ₹47.15 lakh20 years: about ₹91.12 lakh25 years: about ₹1.69 crore

Your own money contributed over those periods would be ₹12 lakh, ₹18 lakh, ₹24 lakh and ₹30 lakh respectively. The difference between contributions and projected value is the modelled investment growth.

Why the later years look so powerful

Compounding has more time to act on the accumulated balance. The portfolio in year 20 contains not only your contributions but also prior growth, and later growth is applied to that larger balance.

What happens at a lower return assumption?

At a 10% annual assumption, the same ₹10,000 monthly investment would project to about ₹20.0 lakh after 10 years, ₹39.8 lakh after 15 years, ₹71.8 lakh after 20 years and ₹1.23 crore after 25 years. The monthly investment did not change; only the assumed return did.

Use ranges: compare several return assumptions instead of treating one future value as an expected outcome.

Does a Monthly Investment (SIP) guarantee returns?

No. A Monthly Investment (SIP) describes a contribution method, not an investment return. Actual outcomes depend on the investment selected, market performance, fees, taxes and the timing of returns. Calculator outputs are illustrative, not guaranteed.

What about inflation?

A future corpus should be interpreted in future purchasing-power terms. If your goal is stated in today's money, inflate the goal first. Otherwise a large future nominal number can create a false sense of security.

What if you increase the monthly investment each year?

Increasing the monthly contribution annually can change the ending value substantially. But a 10% yearly step-up also means the monthly commitment becomes much larger over time, so the future contribution path should be realistic.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the 25-year value much larger than the 20-year value?

The portfolio has five extra years of contributions and five extra years for the accumulated balance to compound. Long horizons make the effect of time increasingly visible.

Are taxes and fees included?

The illustrative figures in this article do not model taxes, product fees or other costs. Those can reduce real-world outcomes.

What if I stop or reduce the SIP later?

The result changes because the contribution path changes. A calculator should be updated whenever the planned monthly amount, step-up or investment period changes.
