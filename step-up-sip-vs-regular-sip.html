STEP-UP SIP GUIDE

Step-up SIP vs regular SIP: how much difference can annual increases make?

A step-up SIP starts at one monthly amount and increases it periodically, while a regular SIP keeps the contribution fixed.

14
FIXED VS INCREASING CONTRIBUTIONS

A step-up SIP changes the contribution path, not the return assumption.

With a regular SIP, the monthly contribution remains fixed. With a step-up SIP, the contribution increases by a chosen percentage, typically once a year. The return assumption can be identical in both cases; the difference comes from putting more money to work later.

Illustration: starting SIP ₹10,000, 20 years, 12% annual return assumptionFixed SIP projected value: about ₹91.1 lakh10% annual step-up projected value: about ₹1.85 croreIn year 20, the monthly SIP would be about ₹61,000.

This example uses Carrowmont's equivalent monthly compound rate and increases the SIP once each year. It is an illustration, not a return forecast.

Why can the gap become so large?

The step-up path contributes much more money over time. Those larger later contributions also have some time to grow. The result therefore reflects both higher contributions and investment growth, not a free return advantage.

The affordability question matters

A 10% annual step-up sounds modest in year one, but it compounds too. A ₹10,000 monthly SIP becomes roughly ₹25,900 in year 11 and about ₹61,000 in year 20. Before using a large step-up in a plan, ask whether income is realistically expected to support that contribution path.

When a step-up can be useful

It can make sense as a planning scenario when someone expects income to rise and prefers to start with a lower contribution. It can also show how directing part of future raises toward long-term goals changes the outcome.

When a fixed SIP may be easier

A fixed contribution is simpler to budget and may be more realistic when income is uncertain or when other financial commitments are expected to rise. The best modelling choice is the one that reflects a plausible future contribution pattern.

Compare apples with apples: when evaluating fixed and step-up SIPs, keep the return assumption, starting amount and time horizon the same. Then the effect of the step-up is easier to understand.

Do not confuse step-up with higher returns

The step-up does not make the investment itself earn more. It increases how much you invest. Market returns remain uncertain.

Frequently asked questions

What is a reasonable annual step-up?

There is no universal percentage. The useful rate is one that fits expected income growth and other obligations. Test several step-ups and check the future monthly amount before choosing a scenario.

Does a step-up SIP reduce investment risk?

No. It changes how much you contribute, not the risk of the underlying investment. Market risk remains determined by the assets you own.

Can I pause a step-up in the model?

Real contribution paths can change. If income or obligations change, update the assumed step-up or switch to a fixed contribution rather than treating the original path as permanent.
