MONTHLY INVESTMENT GUIDE

How much should you invest each month to reach a financial goal?

Instead of starting with a monthly amount and hoping it is enough, work backward from the goal, time horizon and assumptions.

WORK BACKWARD

The monthly amount is an output of the goal, not the starting point.

A common planning mistake is to pick a comfortable monthly amount first and then assume it will fund the goal. A clearer approach is to define the goal amount, the date, any savings already available and a reasonable range of growth assumptions. Then solve for the monthly contribution.

Illustrative target: 100,000 in 10 yearsAt a 6% annual growth assumption, the required monthly contribution is about 615.At an 8% annual growth assumption, it is about 555.These figures use an equivalent monthly compound rate and end-of-month contributions.

The difference shows why return assumptions affect the answer. It also shows why a lower monthly figure is not necessarily "better" if it depends on a more optimistic assumption.

Five inputs usually drive the result

Target amount. What do you actually want available at the goal date?

Time horizon. More time generally lowers the monthly amount because there are more contributions and more time for growth.

Existing savings. Money already set aside for the goal can reduce the amount that new contributions need to fund.

Growth assumption. A higher assumed return lowers the calculated contribution but increases dependence on investment performance.

Inflation. If the goal is stated in today's money, it may need to be grown to a future nominal amount first.

Why time is often the easiest variable to underestimate

Using the same 100,000 target and a 6% annual assumption, a five-year horizon requires roughly 1,439 per month, a 10-year horizon about 615, and a 15-year horizon about 349. The target did not change. The time available did.

Planning insight: if the required monthly amount feels unrealistic, the useful response is not automatically to assume a higher return. You can also revisit the goal amount, deadline, existing resources or contribution path.

Should contributions stay fixed forever?

Not necessarily. Some people prefer a fixed contribution. Others plan to increase the amount as income rises. A step-up contribution can materially change the ending value, but it also means future monthly commitments become larger. Any step-up assumption should therefore be checked against expected affordability.

What if you have several goals?

Separate them. A home deposit in five years and retirement in 30 years have different time horizons, risk capacity and liquidity needs. Combining every goal into one account can make it hard to see whether each target is actually funded.

Frequently asked questions

Should I invest the same amount every month?

A fixed amount is simple, but a planned annual increase can reflect rising income. The important question is whether the future contribution path is realistic and sustainable.

What if my goal date is flexible?

Time is one of the strongest levers. Extending the deadline can reduce the monthly amount because you make more contributions and earlier contributions have longer to grow.

Should I include money already saved?

Yes, if it is genuinely earmarked for the goal. Existing savings can materially reduce the amount new monthly contributions need to fund.
