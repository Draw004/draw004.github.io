Before relying on the headline

    WHAT THIS NUMBER MEANS

    Affordability starts with cash flow, not with the biggest loan available.

    Carrowmont starts with monthly take-home income. It then keeps aside the essential spending, existing debt, savings you want to protect, other ownership costs and the amount you want left free. Only the remaining amount is used for the modeled principal-and-interest payment.

    Monthly room for the loan paymentTake-home income − essentials − existing debt − protected savings − free-cash buffer − other ownership costs

    The calculator then works backward from that monthly payment using the interest rate and term you entered. A larger deposit reduces the loan needed. A higher interest rate reduces the loan that fits the same monthly payment. A longer term can lower the monthly payment, but it usually keeps the debt for longer and can increase total interest.

    Why other ownership costs matter

    A mortgage or home-loan payment is not the whole housing budget. Property tax or rates, insurance, service charges, HOA or strata costs and maintenance can materially change what the home feels like each month. Carrowmont does not invent country-specific amounts; enter the costs that are relevant to the property you are considering.

    Why lender approval can be different

    Lenders can consider verified income, credit history, current debt, property value, loan-to-value rules, stress rates, product rules and local regulation. HOME1 does not reproduce those underwriting systems. Its job is narrower: help you see whether the housing commitment fits the monthly life you described.

    What moves this number?

    • Larger deposit: usually reduces the loan needed.
    • Higher rate: raises the payment for the same loan and reduces borrowing room.
    • Longer term: can reduce the monthly payment but keeps debt for longer.
    • Existing debt: leaves less monthly room for housing.
    • Ownership costs: reduce the price that fits the same budget.
    • More protected savings or buffer: lowers the modeled price but preserves more room for other priorities.
