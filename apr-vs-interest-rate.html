APR vs interest rate: Why the cheaper-looking loan may not be cheaper

Separate the advertised rate from broader cost measures, fees, term and the payment pattern before comparing loans.

By CarrowmontPublished and reviewed 20 October 2026Educational guideEditorial Policy
COMPARE A LOANOpen the Loan / EMI Calculator→

Model the principal, rate, term and recurring repayment, then add disclosed fees separately when comparing the full cash cost.

30-second answer

The interest rate mainly describes the price charged on the borrowed balance. APR, comparison rate or a similar local measure may combine the rate with certain fees and assumptions to show a broader annualised cost. The exact legal calculation differs by country and product, and it may not include every personal cost. Compare the same loan type, amount and term, then read the actual disclosure and total repayment.

India commonly uses APR in the Key Facts Statement, Australia uses comparison rates for many consumer loans, and other markets have their own disclosure rules. Similar labels do not always use identical formulas.

Interest rate and APR answer different questions

MeasureMain question it answersWhat may be missing
Interest rateWhat percentage is charged on the loan balance?Origination, administration, broker or other fees.
APR / annual percentage rateWhat annualised borrowing cost results after including defined charges?Costs excluded by local rules, optional services and future variable-rate changes.
Comparison rate or equivalentHow can a standardised example help compare rate plus common fees?Your actual amount, term or fees may differ from the standard example.
Total repaymentHow much scheduled cash will leave over the stated term?Future rate changes, missed-payment charges or early settlement unless modelled.

No single number tells the whole story. The interest rate helps explain the loan's ongoing price. A broader disclosure helps expose certain fees. The payment and total repayment show the cash-flow effect. The contract explains what can change.

What APR may include

Depending on local rules and the product, APR may include the interest rate plus mandatory origination, service, administrative, broker or other finance charges. Some charges may be excluded, and the assumptions used to annualise the cost can differ.

That is why this guide says APR may include fees rather than claiming it includes every cost worldwide. Insurance chosen separately, late fees, early repayment charges, valuation costs, taxes or future variable-rate changes may sit outside the displayed measure.

Worked example: Lower interest rate, higher overall cost

Suppose two fixed loans each provide 500,000 over 36 months.

The lower rate did not produce the lower combined cost because the fee was much larger. A properly calculated APR or local comparison measure is designed to make this type of difference more visible. The exact disclosed percentages would depend on local rules and timing.

The example assumes both loans are kept for 36 months and payments are made as scheduled. Early repayment can make an upfront fee more important because it has less time to be offset by rate savings.

Term length can dominate the comparison

A lower payment may come from a longer term rather than a better price. Stretching repayment can reduce monthly pressure but keep the balance outstanding for longer. Compare offers over the same loan amount and term before deciding that one rate is cheaper.

If the terms differ, look at both versions: The proposed payment and what the offer would cost over the same term as the alternative. This prevents a term extension from being mistaken for a pure rate saving.

Fixed, variable and introductory rates

  • Fixed rate: The stated rate stays fixed for an agreed period, subject to the contract.
  • Variable rate: The rate may move with an index, benchmark or lender decision allowed by the agreement.
  • Introductory rate: A temporary rate that changes after a set period or event.

An APR calculated from today's variable rate cannot promise the future rate. Likewise, a promotional APR may be useful only if the balance is repaid before the promotion ends. Compare the reset rate, not just the headline.

Balloon, residual and irregular payments

A small regular payment can hide a large final balloon or residual amount. Check whether the disclosure assumes that final payment and whether the asset may need to be sold or refinanced. Also check payment frequency: Weekly, fortnightly and monthly figures should be compared on a consistent annual basis.

What APR may not tell you

  • Whether the payment fits your household budget.
  • How a variable rate will move in the future.
  • What happens if you miss a payment.
  • The value or cost of optional insurance or add-on services.
  • Your personal early-settlement or refinancing date.
  • Whether a secured loan puts an important asset at risk.
  • Whether one lender will approve the application.

A practical comparison process

  1. Compare the same amount, product type and term.
  2. Record the nominal interest rate and whether it is fixed, variable or promotional.
  3. Record APR, comparison rate or the relevant local disclosure.
  4. List every upfront, ongoing and exit fee.
  5. Check payment frequency, number of payments and any balloon amount.
  6. Calculate the total scheduled repayment.
  7. Test a shorter holding period if you may repay or refinance early.
  8. Check the rate-reset and missed-payment rules.
  9. Place the payment in the household budget with a realistic buffer.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing an interest rate with another lender's APR: Compare like with like.
  • Assuming APR includes everything: Local rules define included charges.
  • Ignoring term: A longer term can raise total interest even at a lower rate.
  • Ignoring an upfront fee because it is financed: Adding it to the balance can also add interest.
  • Treating a variable APR as a cap: The future rate may change.
  • Choosing the lowest disclosed rate without checking fit: Flexibility, security and affordability also matter.

Questions to ask before signing

  • Which fees are included in APR or the comparison rate, and which are excluded?
  • What amount and term were assumed?
  • Is the rate fixed, variable or introductory?
  • What is the total scheduled repayment and final payment?
  • Can I repay early, and what will it cost?
  • Does the loan require security or a guarantor?
  • What happens after a missed payment?
  • Can I take the disclosure away and compare it with another offer?

Frequently asked questions

Is APR always higher than the interest rate?

It often is when mandatory fees are included, but the relationship depends on the local calculation and product. Do not rely on that rule without reading the disclosure.

Does the lowest APR always mean the best loan?

No. It is an important cost measure, but term, variable-rate risk, repayment flexibility, security and household affordability also matter.

Can I compare a mortgage APR with a credit-card APR?

Both annualise cost, but the products, assumptions and fee rules differ. APR is most useful when comparing similar products under the same disclosure framework.
