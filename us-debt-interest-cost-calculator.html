HOW TO READ IT

What drives the result?

The model separates the original starting debt into a legacy-rate portion and a portion that has refinanced at the selected new rate. Each year, a fixed tranche of the original debt moves to the selected refinancing rate. The primary deficit and modeled interest cost are then added to debt at year-end and begin accruing interest in the following modeled year.

Year t modeled interest(legacy debt × existing rate) + (new/refinanced debt × selected rate)

The model deliberately uses a transparent beginning-of-year interest / year-end borrowing convention. Real Treasury issuance, coupons, bills, TIPS adjustments and maturity schedules are more complex.

Why refinancing takes time

A move in market yields does not instantly reset the rate on every dollar of outstanding debt. Treasury securities mature over different horizons, so the average cost of the debt stock can move with a lag.

Average cost is not one market yield

The existing average rate in this model is different from a current 10-year Treasury yield or the federal funds rate. It is a simplified average-cost assumption applied to the still-unrefinanced portion of the starting debt.

Debt held by the public vs total public debt

Debt held by the public is Treasury debt held outside federal government accounts. Total Public Debt Outstanding also includes intragovernmental holdings. Carrowmont defaults to debt held by the public because that measure is commonly used in budget and economic analysis.

Primary deficit vs total deficit

The primary deficit excludes net interest. Asking for a primary deficit prevents the model from adding interest twice: the model calculates interest separately and then adds both primary deficit and modeled interest to year-end debt.

Modeled interest vs official net interest

Carrowmont’s modeled interest cost is a simplified scenario output. It is not Treasury-reported gross interest expense and it is not CBO net interest outlays, which use official budget-accounting definitions and offsets.

Why interest can compound the burden

When deficits and interest costs are financed with additional borrowing, the debt stock can grow. In later years that larger stock can itself create more interest cost under the model.

GLOBAL CONTEXT

How could U.S. debt-service conditions matter beyond the U.S.?

Higher or more volatile Treasury borrowing costs can interact with global bond yields, dollar funding conditions, investor risk appetite and capital flows. Those channels may influence borrowing costs and asset prices elsewhere, but the direction and size of any effect depend on many forces.

For India, relevant transmission channels can include global yields, the U.S. dollar, capital flows, the rupee, imported energy costs and domestic financial conditions. This calculator does not predict any of those outcomes.

Keep scenario and prediction separate

A rising modeled interest bill does not by itself imply a default, a dollar collapse, higher gold prices or falling equities. Those outcomes depend on policy, growth, inflation, investor demand and many other variables.

FAQ

Common questions

What is debt held by the public?

It is federal debt held by investors and entities outside federal government accounts, subject to Treasury definitions. The figure includes Treasury securities held by individuals, institutions, governments and Federal Reserve Banks.

How is total public debt different?

Total Public Debt Outstanding equals debt held by the public plus intragovernmental holdings.

Why doesn’t a higher Treasury yield immediately apply to all U.S. debt?

Outstanding securities keep their existing terms until they mature or otherwise roll over. New market rates affect the debt stock gradually as borrowing is issued or refinanced.

What is the average interest rate on federal debt?

It is an average borrowing-cost concept across the debt stock, not one current Treasury yield. CBO estimated the average interest rate on debt held by the public at 3.4% in 2026.

Why does this calculator use a primary deficit?

Because primary deficit excludes net interest. The model calculates interest separately, avoiding double counting.

Is modeled interest cost the same as CBO net interest outlays?

No. This is a simplified deterministic model. CBO net interest is an official budget measure with specific accounting treatment and offsets.

Does this calculator predict a U.S. default?

No. It is an educational sensitivity model, not a default, market or Treasury-yield forecast.

Does the Federal Reserve directly set the interest rate on all Treasury debt?

No. The Federal Reserve sets a target range for the federal funds rate. Treasury securities are issued across maturities and their market yields reflect many forces.

Why can interest expense keep rising even if rates stop rising?

If existing lower-rate debt continues to mature into a higher-rate environment, the average cost can keep moving upward. Additional borrowing can also expand the debt stock.

How could U.S. debt-service conditions affect India?

Possible channels include global yields, the dollar, capital flows, the rupee, imported energy costs and domestic financing conditions. The calculator does not predict direction or magnitude.

Why are all calculations in USD?

The tool models U.S. federal debt. It does not perform exchange-rate conversion or relabel dollar debt in another currency.

Is this investment advice?

No. The tool is educational scenario analysis and does not recommend securities, portfolios or market trades.
