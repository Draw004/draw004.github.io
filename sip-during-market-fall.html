MONTHLY INVESTMENT (SIP) GUIDE

What happens to a SIP when markets fall?

A fixed recurring investment buys more units when the investment price is lower and fewer when it is higher, but that does not make market losses disappear.

In India, recurring mutual-fund investing is commonly called a SIP. Outside India, Carrowmont uses the broader recurring-investment language.

What changes when the market price falls?

If you invest the same amount on a fixed schedule, a lower price buys more units and a higher price buys fewer. AMFI describes this as rupee-cost averaging for SIP investors; Investor.gov describes the same general mechanism as dollar-cost averaging.

Simple unit-price illustrationFixed contribution: 1,000 currency unitsAt a unit price of 20: 50 units purchasedAt a unit price of 10: 100 units purchasedTotal: 2,000 invested for 150 units, average purchase cost about 13.33 per unit

A lower average purchase cost is not a profit guarantee

AMFI explicitly notes that rupee-cost averaging does not assure profit and does not protect against investment losses in declining markets. If the underlying investment keeps falling or fails to recover, the portfolio can still lose money.

Regular investing mainly changes the timing problem

A recurring plan avoids making one large decision about the perfect entry date. It spreads purchases across many market levels. SEBI investor education specifically addresses the question of continuing SIPs through volatile markets, while the U.S. Investor.gov definition describes dollar-cost averaging as equal investments at regular intervals regardless of market ups and downs.

When should the plan itself be reviewed?

Market movement alone is not the only consideration. Revisit the plan if the goal, time horizon, emergency liquidity, debt position, risk tolerance or underlying investment has changed. A contribution schedule should not force you to ignore short-term financial stability.

What the Carrowmont calculator can and cannot show

Carrowmont can illustrate how recurring contributions, time and an assumed annual return interact. It does not model daily market prices, volatility, sequence of returns, fund selection, taxes or fees. A smooth projection should therefore not be interpreted as the path an actual investment will take.

Frequently asked questions

Does a market fall make a SIP better?

A lower price means the same contribution buys more units, but whether that leads to a better eventual outcome depends on the investment and future market performance.

Should I increase a SIP during a fall?

That is an individual investment and cash-flow decision. This educational guide does not recommend changing contributions based on short-term market movements.

Can I lose money even with a long SIP?

Yes. Regular investing does not guarantee returns. The selected investment, fees, taxes and market performance remain important.
