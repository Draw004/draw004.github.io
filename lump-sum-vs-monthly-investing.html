Lump sum vs monthly investing: how does timing change the maths?

The timing of when money enters a projection changes how long each amount has to compound.

WANT TO START WITH YOUR NUMBERS?Model existing savings plus monthly investments→

Prefer to understand the assumptions first? Start with the basics below, then return to the calculator with more context.

BEFORE YOU START

The key difference is time in the model.

A lump sum invested at the beginning has the full period to compound. Monthly contributions enter gradually, so later contributions have less time in the projection.

This is mathematics, not a recommendation about market timing.

Real markets move unpredictably. The example isolates contribution timing under a smooth return assumption so you can see why the projected values differ.

Existing savings and new contributions should be modelled separately.

If you already have money invested and also plan monthly contributions, projecting those two streams separately makes the result easier to understand.

Risk tolerance and liquidity are different questions.

A mathematical comparison does not decide whether an amount should be invested, kept liquid or spread over time. Those decisions depend on circumstances outside the calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the lump-sum projection higher in a constant positive-return model?

Because more of the money is exposed to the assumed growth rate for longer. That does not mean the same outcome will occur in real markets.

Can I model both at once?

Yes. Carrowmont's monthly investment calculator allows an existing invested amount and recurring monthly contributions to be included together.
